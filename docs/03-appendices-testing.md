# Appendices — Usability Testing Materials (templates only)

> **Nothing in this file is data.** Every table is blank on purpose. Do not enter or invent participants, times, scores or quotations. Fill these in only from sessions that actually take place. Check with your instructor whether student usability testing needs ethics or supervisor approval before you recruit anyone.

## Ethics and privacy principles

- Voluntary participation; participants may stop at any time without giving a reason.
- Participants use only the fictional demo data. **Never ask for, or let anyone type, a real PIN, password, OTP, card number or account number.**
- Collect the minimum: participant code (P1, P2…), age band, preferred language, smartphone/finance-app experience. No names on result sheets. No audio, video or photos unless the participant separately agrees in writing.
- Keep the consent sheets (which carry names) separate from the result sheets (which carry only codes). Store both securely; delete when the assignment is marked, unless the instructor requires otherwise.
- Do not recruit anyone who would feel pressured (for example a dependent, or someone who cannot easily say no). Tell participants it is the *prototype* being tested, not them.
- Provide the session in the participant's preferred language. Read everything aloud for people who prefer not to read.

## Recruitment plan

Aim for **5–8 participants** across the three target groups (older adults with limited finance-app experience; people with low digital or reading literacy; Urdu-preferring adults uncomfortable with English interfaces). Recruit through family, neighbours, community or mosque/church notice boards, and adult-learning or literacy groups, with permission from whoever manages the venue. Try to include variety in age, vision, hearing and smartphone familiarity. Record how many people you approached and how many agreed. Do not include classmates who already know the prototype as "target users"; they can serve as a separate pilot of the procedure.

## Appendix A — Test tasks

Say each scenario aloud. Do not explain the interface. If the participant is stuck for more than about 60 seconds, give the **smallest** hint, record it as an assistance request, and move on after a second hint.

| # | Scenario (read aloud, in the participant's language) | Start page | Success means |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | "Your electricity bill is due. Pay it using your voice, or by typing if you prefer." | Voice assistant | Reaches the DEMO receipt for the electricity bill |

## Appendix B — Consent script (read aloud; adapt language)

"Thank you for helping. I am a student at Bahria University studying how easy a mobile app is to use. The app is a **practice version**. It has **no real money, no real bank and no real accounts**. Everything on screen is made up.

I will ask you to try five small tasks. We are testing the app, **not you**. If something is hard, that tells me the app needs improving. You can stop at any time, skip any task or question, and you do not have to give a reason. Stopping will not affect anything.

I will write notes about what you do and say, using a code such as P3 instead of your name. I will **not** record your voice or take photos unless you separately agree. Please do **not** type any real PIN, password, OTP, card number or bank details. The notes will be used only for my university assignment, kept private, and deleted after marking.

Do you have any questions? Do you agree to take part?"

## Appendix C3 — Post-test questionnaire

Read aloud if needed. Use a 5-point scale with faces or words (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree). Provide it in Urdu and English.

1. It was easy to do the tasks.
2. I felt confident that I was doing the right thing.
3. I always understood what the app was doing (for example, listening or waiting).
4. When something went wrong, I knew what to do next.
5. I understood the words the app used.
6. I felt safe because I could check the details and stop before paying.
7. I would be comfortable using something like this again.
8. Speaking to the app felt natural. *(skip if the participant did not use voice)*
9. The text and buttons were easy to see and press.

Open question: "What was the most confusing part? What did you like most?"

*Optional:* the ten-item System Usability Scale (Brooke, 1996) in a translation you have checked for clarity. Only describe it as administered if you actually used it, and report the translation you used.

## Appendix C4 — Short interview guide (5 minutes)

1. Tell me in your own words what you did when you paid the bill.
2. Was there a moment you were unsure what to do or what the app was doing? What happened?
3. How did you feel when the app showed the details before paying? Was it clear what would happen?
4. How did speaking compare with typing or buttons for you?
5. Were the Urdu words and the English words easy to understand? Anything you would change?
6. If your family member were using this, what would worry you?
7. Anything else?

## Success criteria (define before testing; adjust only with a reason you record)

| Measure | How calculated | Suggested target for a first prototype |
|---|---|---|
| Task completion rate | (tasks completed without help ÷ tasks attempted) × 100, per task and overall; report "with help" separately | ≥ 80% overall; no task below 60% |
| Time on task (T1 and T2) | Median seconds from first action to receipt, successful attempts only; report range | Set a target from your own pilot of the procedure, not from a published norm |
| Errors and assistance | Mean errors and mean assistance requests per task | ≤ 1 error and ≤ 1 request per task on average |
| Ease and confidence | Mean and range of questionnaire items 1 and 2 | Mean ≥ 4.0 |
| Safety understanding | Number of participants who state that nothing real was paid and that they pressed the final button themselves | All participants |

These targets are suggestions, not published standards. Choose and justify your own.

## Analysis method

1. Enter every sheet into the blank tables below using participant codes.
2. Calculate each measure exactly as defined above. Report counts alongside percentages (for example "4 of 6"), because the sample is small.
3. List each observed problem once with: task, what happened, number of participants affected, severity (1 = cosmetic, 2 = minor, 3 = major, 4 = blocks the task), and the principle it relates to.
4. Group open answers and interview notes into themes; quote only words you recorded with consent.
5. For each severity 3–4 problem, propose a design change, and mark it **proposed** or **implemented**. Add implemented changes to the requirements table's "Change after feedback" column.
6. State limitations: sample size, recruitment, setting, device and browser, whether voice worked, and who ran the sessions. Do not claim statistical significance or that results generalise.
